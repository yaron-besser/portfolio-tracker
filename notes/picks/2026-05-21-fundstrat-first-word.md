## 1 · April FOMC minutes — hawkish bias if inflation persists

**Three pillars from the minutes:**

| Theme | Key takeaway |
|---|---|
| **Rates** | "A majority of participants highlighted, however, that some policy firming would likely become appropriate if inflation were to continue to run persistently above 2 percent." Many participants would have preferred removing the easing-bias language from the post-meeting statement. **Hike is now on the table** if inflation sticks. |
| **Oil** | "Effects on economic activity of the recent increase in oil prices may be smaller than those seen in the past, citing factors such as the relatively large amount of current domestic oil production or the relatively low share of current domestic spending on oil." Explicit "this time is different" framing — pushback on historical comparisons. |
| **AI** | "Several participants observed that price pressures associated with strong AI investment expenditures would likely raise input costs for a range of industries." Also: firms delaying/reducing hiring in anticipation of AI adoption could push unemployment "sharply higher." AI is now showing up in **both** Fed mandates — inflation pressure AND labor slack risk. |

**March → April shift (per Fundstrat comparison table):**

| Dimension | March 17–18 | April 28–29 |
|---|---|---|
| Dissents | Single dovish dissent (Miran) | **Four** — Miran dovish + Hammack/Kashkari/Logan rejecting easing bias. Hawkish bloc emerges. |
| Forward bias | "Default path = cuts" | **"Hike now on table"** if inflation persists |
| AI narrative | AI as **downside** risk; software underperformed | AI as **inflation driver**; tech recorded strongest gains |
| Oil pass-through | Generic framing | Explicit "this time is different" pushback on historical comparisons |
| Private credit | Manager-level mention | **Formal FSR-level** discussion; signs of stabilization |
| Staff outlook | "Not as strong as January" | **"Slightly stronger than March"** — growth AND inflation revised up |

**Read:** The directional bias has shifted from "default cut" to "watching to see if hike is needed." This is consistent with the message Mark Newton has been delivering tactically — yields could break out and pressure equities into late May / early June (already operational textbook in tactical_playbook.md).
