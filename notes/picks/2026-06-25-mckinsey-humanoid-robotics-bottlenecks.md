## The framework

> "Supply chain bottlenecks are most likely to emerge around actuators and sensing and perception systems."

McKinsey's risk taxonomy for ~25 component categories:

| Risk | Meaning | Components flagged |
|---|---|---|
| 🟪 **HIGH** | Structural constraints will emerge | **NdFeB magnets** (in motors), **Planetary roller screws**, **6-axis force/torque sensors** |
| 🟦 **MEDIUM** | Scaled supply in adjacent industries, more extensive adaptation needed | Encoders (rotary/linear), Linear force sensors, Linear actuator integration, Real-time control platforms, Safety controls, Driver board/power electronics, Brushless DC + permanent magnet synchronous motors, Bearings (cross-roller), Cycloidal drives |
| 🟩 **LOW** | Scaled supply already exists, light adaptation | AI compute/GPU modules, Battery packs, Thermal/cooling, Vision (LiDAR/depth), Tactile sensors, Ball screws, Linear guides, Planetary gearboxes, Harmonic/strain-wave drives, Frames/housing, Precision mechanical parts, Commodity mechanical |

## Where the alpha actually sits

**The bottlenecks (HIGH risk) are where pricing power emerges.** McKinsey's argument: scarcity in actuators + sensors will compound as humanoid production scales (Fundstrat target: 1M humanoids by 2030).

The catch: **most genuinely scarce names are private or non-US-listed.** Public-equity access to this thesis is thinner than the "power as bottleneck" thesis.
