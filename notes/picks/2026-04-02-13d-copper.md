## What it says

**Performance**
- The 13D Copper Index rose 4.4% in Q1 2026, against a 4.3% loss for the S&P
  500 and a 3.5% loss for the MSCI World Index.   p.45

**The supply case**
- "The mounting fundamental and technical evidence suggests that copper's price
  gains are likely to be durable." They read it as a signal that new supply has
  to come online, or demand be curtailed, to balance the market.   p.45
- Their problem with that: "new supply cannot be brought on fast enough or
  cheaply enough, while global economic and technology trends will require huge
  volumes of incremental supply."   p.45
- "Accessing copper is increasingly going to become a matter of national
  security and governments ultimately will pay any price necessary to get their
  hands on the metal."   p.45
- They cite an S&P Global study from January, "Copper in the Age of AI:
  Challenges of Electrification": primary copper supply peaks in 2030, and a
  10 million tonne supply/demand gap opens by 2040.   p.45

**Why they think the miners work even if oil rises**
- Commodities "tend to rise and fall together, with gold leading, base metals
  following gold and oil eventually following base metals". Through the end of
  this decade they expect rising commodity prices to more than offset miners'
  cost inflation from oil.   p.46
- Their precedent: they recommended copper and copper miners in May 2003. Oil
  then rose five-fold to its $147 peak in 2008, and Freeport McMoRan and Pan
  American Silver still appreciated 4x to 6x by their 2008 highs.   p.46
