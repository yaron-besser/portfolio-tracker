## 13D's thesis

- **Earth's energy imbalance accelerating** — extra heat since 1960s = 430+
  zettajoules. Oceans absorbed 23 zettajoules last year alone (= 37 years
  of global power production)
- **Disaster costs doubling** — US annual disaster spending up from <$500B
  in 2013 to ~$1T now. $6.6T over 12 years = 2x the Great Depression's
  inflation-adjusted loss
- **Climate migration accelerating** — 11M Americans relocated due to
  natural disasters including Hurricanes Milton + Helene (2024)
- **Super El Niño likely** — 62% probability El Niño in June-Aug 2026;
  20-25% chance of Super El Niño by late 2026 (80% strong-event likelihood)
- **2027 projected hottest year in 125,000 years**
- **"Climate change will reshape economic structures themselves"** —
  where people live, what's produced, infrastructure viability, regional
  economic viability
- **Damage models systematically underestimate risk** — fail to account
  for tipping points + extreme events
- Risks: drought across US West, Central Asia, Southern Europe;
  agricultural disruption (links to [02-food-security.md](02-food-security.md))

## What could be wrong

- **Climate is a 30-100 year thesis, not a 1-year investment thesis.** Hard
  to extract specific tradeable signals from secular climate change without
  conflating with cyclical weather
- **El Niño probabilities are NOAA model outputs, not certainties.** Models
  have been wrong before (e.g., predicted Atlantic hurricane seasons that
  underperformed)
- **Damage cost numbers conflate inflation, population growth, and increasing
  property values in disaster-prone areas with climate signal.** Real cost
  per disaster (constant dollars, constant population) is less alarming
- **Insurance market is the actual transmission mechanism.** Climate-induced
  insurer pullback (FL, CA already happening) creates real estate
  dislocations — but these are slow-moving, not Q1-2026 actionable
- **Many "climate" stocks are renewables proxies** (see 12-disruption-ai.md)
  — they trade on policy + interest rates more than climate fundamentals
- **Government adaptation spending is real but politically variable.**
  Trump admin pulled back federal climate funding. State-level + private
  capital continues but at lower pace
