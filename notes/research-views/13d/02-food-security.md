Source: Q1-2026 Review.

---

## 13D's thesis

- **Food production is structurally at risk** from extreme weather, drought,
  declining arable land, soil erosion, plateauing grain yields — a thesis
  13D has held for 20+ years
- **Strait of Hormuz closure compounds this**: Gulf states = 49% global urea,
  30% global ammonia. Fertilizer supply broken.
- **Russia/Ukraine war still ongoing**: ~25% of global wheat trade affected
- **Super El Niño likely**: US Climate Prediction Center = 62% probability
  El Niño in June-Aug 2026, 20-25% chance of "Super El Niño" by late 2026
- **Higher diesel = higher food prices** (tractors, combines, shipping). US
  diesel +42% late-Feb to late-March vs +35% gasoline
- **Corn planting estimates revised down** as farmers switch to soybeans
  (less nitrogen-fertilizer dependent)
- **400 million people across MENA + East Africa** dealing with supply shock

## Key claims / data points

- **Gulf region ≈ 1/3 of global fertilizer exports.** Strait of Hormuz
  blockade = fertilizer prices surging
- **49% global urea, 30% global ammonia from Gulf states** (Chris Krebs, FT)
- **Roughly half of global food supply** passes through Strait of Hormuz
- **"Agricultural-damage clock runs in weeks"** — winter wheat needs final
  nitrogen application in 3-4 weeks (as of March 2026)
- **Energy costs filter through** to all physical goods including food
- **CF Industries (CF) added to HCI portfolio** at 1% weight on 2026-03-27 —
  fertilizer pure-play
- **El Niño impact won't peak until 2027** — projected hottest year in
  125,000 years

## What could be wrong

- **El Niño probabilities are not certainties.** 62% probability ≠ "will
  happen." Climate models have wide error bands.
- **Iran War de-escalation** = fertilizer supply restored quickly. Hormuz
  closure is the *acute* driver; the chronic 20-year thesis hasn't actually
  produced sustained food inflation despite 13D's warnings
- **Fertilizer is highly cyclical.** Last bull (2008, 2022) ended in sharp
  drawdowns. Timing matters more than thesis.
- **Substitution / soybean rotation** is already happening — markets adapt
  faster than the linear extrapolation suggests
- **Government intervention.** US, EU, China all subsidize agriculture
  heavily. A genuine food crisis triggers price controls, export bans,
  emergency reserves — which dampens equity upside even if the underlying
  thesis is right
- **Index composition matters.** Food Security Index includes processors,
  distributors, AND producers. Different parts of the chain respond
  differently to a shock (producers benefit, processors get squeezed)
- **The "20-year conviction"** caveat: 13D has been bullish food security
  for 20 years. The Food Security Index returned +143% in ~5.5 years
  (Oct 2020 to now) — that's good but largely tracked broader commodities.
  Single-year +23.6% Q1 is the actual outperformance
