## 13D's thesis

- **Uranium is in early stages of a new super-cycle bull market** emerging
  from a 12+ year bear market
- **Structural supply deficit of 30-50M lbs** for existing reactor fleet,
  before counting any new reactors
- Only ~116M lbs of long-term contracts vs **>200M lbs replacement level**
- Since 2013, utilities contracted only ~45% of annual consumption
- **75% of annual reactor requirements remain uncontracted through 2040**
- **No major new mine coming online for ~3+ years**
- Inventories at record-low levels in the West
- **Uranium demand is inelastic** — 4-8% of reactor operating cost vs 80-90%
  for fossil fuel in thermal plants. Higher prices don't curtail demand.
- Nuclear renaissance: 30+ countries considering nuclear as central to
  decarbonization
- **Target: $125+/lb** before structural deficit resolves

## What could be wrong

- **Already up 1,562%.** Buying at any-time-highs into a deep cycle requires
  conviction the cycle has years left
- **Substitution / efficiency.** Reactor fuel efficiency continues to improve.
  Demand growth assumption may be aggressive
- **Geopolitical supply.** Kazakhstan (largest producer), Russia, Niger
  dominate uranium supply. A geopolitical de-escalation OR Western producers
  ramping changes the picture
- **Small modular reactor (SMR) reality check.** The nuclear renaissance
  thesis hinges on SMRs scaling. Most SMR projects are still pre-commercial.
  Real fuel demand impact is 5-10 years away
- **Public sentiment is fickle.** Major nuclear incident (anywhere) sets the
  thesis back years politically
- **Index components concentration.** Uranium miners are concentrated
  globally — Cameco, Kazatomprom, NextGen, Denison, Paladin. Idiosyncratic
  risk per name is high
- **Inelastic demand cuts both ways.** Utilities don't reduce demand on
  price spikes — but they DO reduce demand on supply chain disruption /
  reactor outages. Demand is more brittle than the framing suggests
- **Backwardation in long-term contracts.** $90 long-term vs $80 spot is
  unusual — usually long-term is at a discount. Reflects utility scrambling,
  but could normalize down if supply ramps
