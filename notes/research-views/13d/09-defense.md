## 13D's thesis

- **Global arms race accelerating** as new technologies (drones, AI, hypersonics)
  shift the balance of power
- **Iran War + Russia/Ukraine = stockpile depletion** across NATO
- **US faces massive replenishment costs** — Trump requesting $200B to fund
  Iran conflict; FY2027 proposal targets $1.5T defense budget
- Global military spending hit **record $2.63T in 2025** (+2.5% real, per IISS)
- **US-China tech-arms race** intensifying — China leads in scientific
  research for 90% of crucial technologies, narrowing chip-tech gap
- **South Korea emerging as world's most flexible/exportable defense
  superpower** (K9 howitzer, K2 tank, various missile systems)
- **AI warfare, cybersecurity, biodefense, Arctic militarization** all sub-
  themes covered
- "Sovereign agency + security infrastructure + resilience" framing

## What could be wrong

- **Cyclical, not secular.** Defense budgets are political — a new admin or
  a peace deal can crater the sector
- **Iran War resolution** = sharp short-term reset on the replenishment trade
- **Already up +595% since 2016.** Mean reversion risk after a long bull
- **Single-payer customer.** Defense primes (LMT, RTX, NOC, GD) ultimately
  depend on US/NATO budgets. Procurement delays + cost overruns are
  endemic
- **Tech disruption from below.** Drone warfare is showing $500 quadcopters
  can take out $5M tanks. Traditional primes may not be the winners — pure-
  play drone makers (AeroVironment, Anduril private) could be
- **South Korea export risk.** Export-heavy defense models are tariff-
  vulnerable + diplomatically fragile (Saudi sale risks, German conditions)
- **13D's own concession:** they're not the highest-conviction here (1.75% of
  HCI). Treat as a "good not great" allocation per their own positioning
