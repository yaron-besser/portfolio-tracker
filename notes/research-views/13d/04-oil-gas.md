## 13D's thesis

- **20+ year thesis on aging supergiant oilfields + accelerating decline rates**
  (citing their own WILTW June 2005, April 2008)
- Iran War is the **trigger, not the cause** — exposes a vulnerability
  ignored for years
- Conventional wisdom ("oil falls back when Iran calms") is wrong because
  it ignores structural underinvestment
- **IEA September 2025 study of 15,000 fields**: post-peak conventional decline
  rate averages **5.6%**, super-giants 2.7%, Middle East 1.8%, Europe 9.7%
- **45M bpd of new supply needed by 2050** just to maintain current production
- Recent discoveries: **<10B barrels/year** (well below needed)
- **Real upstream investment down 50%+ since 2015**
- **US independents favored** — capital discipline + bullish prices + minimal
  Middle East exposure

## What could be wrong

- **The "peak supply" narrative has been wrong for 20 years.** Shale changed
  this once (2010-2020). Could change again — drilling tech, AI in
  exploration, deep-water, reservoir engineering
- **Demand destruction is the elephant in the room.** EV adoption,
  efficiency, recession all reduce demand. 13D barely engages with this
- **Iran War de-escalation = sharp oil drop.** Even a perceived path to
  resolution would cut $20-30 off Brent overnight
- **US shale is more responsive than 13D suggests.** Permian drilled-but-
  uncompleted wells can come online in 6 months. Saudi has 3M bpd spare
  capacity (officially)
- **High oil prices accelerate the transition.** EV economics get
  dramatically more attractive at $100+ oil, accelerating demand destruction
- **Index composition risk.** 13D Oil & Gas Index components not detailed
  in this report — could be heavy in service / E&P names with idiosyncratic
  risk
- **+45% in one quarter is a lot.** Mean reversion risk significant
- **13D is *most* confident here** — that's when to be most careful about
  confirmation bias
