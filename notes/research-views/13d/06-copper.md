## 13D's thesis

- **Copper price gains are durable** — strong market signal that new supply
  needs to come online or demand must be curtailed
- **New supply can't be brought on fast enough or cheaply enough**
- Demand growth from: AI datacenters, electrification, EVs, grid build-out
- **Governments will pay any price** for security-of-supply
- Historical reference: 2003-2008 bull market saw FCX and PAAS rise 4-6x
  despite oil rising 5x (cost pressure didn't stop the bull)
- Commodities tend to rise together: **gold leads, base metals follow, oil
  follows base metals**. Through end of decade, commodity prices should
  outpace cost inflation for miners

## What could be wrong

- **"Copper deficit" has been called for over a decade.** Prices have ranged
  $2-5/lb in fits and starts. The deficit is real long-term but trading
  it is hard
- **Demand assumptions hinge on EV / grid build-out timing.** If EV
  adoption slows (battery shortages, charging infrastructure gaps), copper
  intensity per GDP unit doesn't grow as projected
- **Substitution risk.** Aluminum substitution in cables (slow but
  meaningful at sustained high prices). Lithium-iron-phosphate batteries use
  less copper than NMC chemistries
- **Recession risk.** Copper is the most cyclical base metal ("Dr. Copper").
  A 2026-27 recession would crater the price even with the long-term thesis
  intact
- **Existing miners can't just "produce more"** — but they CAN increase
  recovery rates, restart marginal mines, expand brownfield. Real supply
  response in 2-3 years from price signal
- **Chinese demand uncertainty.** ~50% of global copper demand. China
  property bust + slower growth = demand risk that 13D doesn't engage with
  here
